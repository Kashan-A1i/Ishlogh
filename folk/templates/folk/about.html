Ishlogh

Archiving the tales that shaped our region

Ishlogh began around a fire, in the voice of a grandmother finishing a story just as the lamp burned low. Growing up, we heard tales like these constantly, ghost stories that made you check the dark corners of the house, myths that explained why a mountain looked a certain way, legends passed down from people who themselves had heard them from someone older still. No one wrote them down. They just moved from mouth to ear, generation to generation, and stayed alive because someone always remembered to tell them again.

Ishlogh exists so that memory doesn't have to carry the whole weight alone.

This is a home for folklore from every region and every people, the ghost stories, the creation myths, the local legends, the half-believed warnings elders give children about staying out after dark. Some of these stories are already fading, known only to the oldest generation in a village or a family. Others are still very much alive, told at gatherings and around dinner tables. Ishlogh is a place to gather them all: to write them down, to give them a permanent home, and to let people everywhere discover the folklore of places far from their own.

This project started with one region's stories and one family's memory of hearing them. It's built to grow into something much bigger, a living archive shaped by anyone willing to share the tales they grew up with, so that the stories that shaped us don't quietly disappear with the people who told them.
